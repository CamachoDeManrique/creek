**Chapter 5**

**Marla- Orbits**

The sky was a brighter blue outside the windows in South Park. Marla wondered if it was because there were not enough tall buildings to block the view. Only the mountains stood as guardians in the distance, streaks of snow running across their faces like veins. In New York, the windows only gave you more buildings. Trees that stretched out asking for attention. Blocking any sky for you to see. 

Her Catholic high school had been three stories tall. Even with that height, the view was still unimpressive. Still, she’d gaze out and look at people passing by. The regulars who used the same street every day. She liked to guess where they were going or what kind of life they lived. Practicing her skills of observation. Whether she was wrong or right, she never found out. She liked the idea of existing alongside the regulars outside the window. 

It made her feel less alone. 

“Craig,” Tolkien whispered. “Dude.”

Marla turned and watched Tolkien gently nudge Craig. 

Craig blinked slowly. The way his eyes had darkened made her wonder just how bad his dream might have been. His eyes met hers, and she realized she had been staring. She turned away and watched as the teacher snapped his laptop shut.

*Finally\! It’s over.* 

Marla slipped her backpack on and glanced behind her. Clyde and Tolkien were talking in front of Craig’s desk. Craig still looked dazed, like he had come out of something unpleasant. She took one last look at the group before stepping into the busy hallway. 

Marla had already begun building profiles for the few boys she met that morning. Her brain moved quickly, feeding on every detail it could collect. Recording small movements, expressions, tones of voices. Anything that would help her remember them later. 

She liked returning to her mind palace and sorting through what she had gathered. 

Clyde: the best friend. The one Tricia said she could go to for answers. He had warm, deep-brown eyes that carried a permanent squint, like he was always halfway into a smirk or plotting a joke. His aloofness was very purposeful. It was why she was caught off guard when he tried to piss off Craig that morning. Then she saw the shift: Craig had been stiff and tired, but the moment the chase started, he relaxed. Clyde knew how to read Craig.

Tolkien appeared to be the only Black student in the morning crowd. From what she gathered, he was the rich friend of the group. A summer trip to Italy and the largest house in South Park were strong hints about his upbringing. He was almost as tall as Craig, his skin a deep cocoa brown that matched his eyes. His black hair was cropped close with a subtle fade above the ears, giving him a neat, understated look. He seemed more mature than the rest of the boys in the way he spoke and carried himself. 

Jimmy had cerebral palsy. Marla only recognized the signs because she had once watched a documentary about a mother taking care of her son with CP. The scissored walk, the jerky movements, and rigid posture. They all matched what she remembered. But the disability and metal forearm crutches didn’t stop him from keeping up with his friends— or from calling her a pretty girl to see her reaction. His light brown hair fell messily across his head, and his round brown eyes carried a playful, joyful energy. 

All three boys stayed close that morning, orbiting around Craig and Tweek. 

*A curious group of boys*. 

Then there was Butters. He was as tall as Tweek, though their hair made them easy to tell apart. Butters’ was a pale golden blond, styled like a soft pineapple—a loose crown of hair that leaned gently to one side. His large blue eyes were also different from Tweek’s. They carried a permanent look of surprise, or gentle curiosity. 

Still, the two boys shared one thing in common: eye trauma. 

*One eye is lighter than the other. Fascinating.*

The only thing she couldn’t really pinpoint yet was Butters’ place in the group. He had walked away from them once they reached the stairs. A clear sign he belonged somewhere else socially. His nervous twitches were also a clue she couldn't ignore; there had to be some past trauma there. But his bright personality refused to let whatever it was win. Could his trauma be attached to his scar? 

*If his story is true, surviving a ninja star to the eye with minimal damage is a miracle. Scar is awesome though.*

She wanted more details but wasn’t able to ask during their morning tour. Butters had taught her about the locker numbers. If the locker starts with a 1, you’re on the first floor. Locker numbers starting with a 2 or a 3 are found on the second floor. Probably due to late registration, she was placed wherever there was a free locker. Locker 133\. She walked downstairs, unable to help the smile that tugged at her lips as she approached it.

*Top locker\! I always wanted a top locker\!* 

Bottom lockers were Marla’s curse. Her kryptonite. Her Moriarty. All through her middle school and first year of high school she’d lost the locker lottery and ended up on the bottom row next to the most popular people. Their lockers would always be swarmed, and she would be bumped around. Things would also drop on her head almost all the time. The heaviest thing to fall on her was a laptop. 

*I really hope you're not stuck. Here we go*. 

She gripped the locker handle and pulled it up. The locker popped open easily. She sighed in relief. If she wasn’t surrounded by people, she would have danced a little. She started to put away her backpack when she suddenly felt arms around her neck.

“Bestie\!”

Marla’s body stiffened. Human contact didn’t bother her; it was *unwanted* human contact that made her rigid. Her grandparents had never hugged her, so something as sudden and affectionate as this made her turn to stone. 

“Hello, Candace,” Marla said without turning around. 

The other profile she had been working on since yesterday was for this girl. *Candace Whitney*. So far, she fit the very typical rich, beautiful, and popular girl profile. There were always girls like Candie in Marla’s schools. None of them were ever very different from the next.

“Geez. Where you been?” Candie let go and leaned against the locker next to her.

“Our teacher let us out late.”

“Ugh. Boring.” Candie peeked inside Marla's locker. “This whole school kind of sucks. I want to go home already.” She turned back to Marla. “I seriously wanted to puke when I saw the whole K-12 sign at the front this morning. How annoying.”

“You didn’t notice it yesterday?”

Candie shrugged and flipped her hair over her shoulder.

*Of course you didn't. You were too busy talking about yourself.* 

“It’s a small town. What did you expect?” Marla said as she took out a black folder from her backpack.

Candie leaned in a little closer, her eyes meeting Marla’s. “I was pretty sad you didn’t introduce me to your cousin this morning.”

“You don't look like the type of person that needs help with introductions.” Marla unzipped the front pocket of her backpack to grab her pencil. “I’m sure you did fine on your own.”

Candie scoffed.

“He ignored me, so just goes to show how I did.” Candie looked down at her perfectly manicured nails. “So, wanna hear what I found out about Craig?” She stroked her blonde hair over her shoulder. “It’s pretty interesting.”

“No.” Marla pushed her backpack farther inside the locker. 

“You won’t be able to figure it out yourself. No one is going to tell you.”

“Why?” Marla hugged the folder close to her chest. “Because we’re related?”

“Sure, that. And well…” Candie started to twirl her hair around her finger. “He’s known for all the wrong reasons. Nobody wants to get on his bad side.”

“And you do?”

“Oh, I’d love to see that.” Candie let out a girlish giggle. “There’s just something about a guy with a dark past, ya know?” Candie placed her hands over her cheeks, gushing. “The more broken the better.”

“And…you’re going to fix him?” Marla raised her eyebrows in disbelief.

*This doesn’t fit the profile. Popular girls go for popular boys, they don't go for charity cases. What’s in it for her?*

“Who said anything about fixing?” Candie dropped her hands.

Her smile became a wide grin, spreading slowly across her face. The grin stretched too wide, making her eyes gleam in a way that made Marla uncomfortable. A feral gleam of something she’d only read about in books. 

*Oh, she isn’t normal.* 

“I think you should leave them alone,” Marla started, but stopped as Candie’s face switched in a fraction of a second. She beamed, calling out a sweet ‘hello’ to another kid passing by. 

*Ok, so that’s her mask. Maybe she’s a bully.* 

“I’m just trying to be friends with Craig and his little buddy, Tweek.” Candie smiled at Marla.

“Boyfriend,” Marla said firmly as she closed the locker with a sharp slam. 

Candie gave her a small dismissive eye roll, the corner of her lips twitching upward. It was the unmistakable look of someone doubting your words.

*She doesn’t think they're dating? Or did she hear something?*

“You know, I think Craig is like those big exotic cats.” Candie placed an index finger on her chin and shifted her weight from one leg to another. “Is it the black panther or jaguar that has green eyes?”

“Black jaguars are more likely to have green eyes. Panther is an umbrella term for the species.”

“Of course you would know that.” She moved her hand to pet Marla’s head. Marla intercepted her wrist and slowly moved it away. “Well yeah, that’s what Craig is. A beautiful black jaguar with those gorgeous, sad green eyes.” A wishful breath escaped Candie’s lips. “I’ve always wanted a big exotic cat.”

“You know people who own exotic animals often get killed by them, right?”

“Isn’t that exciting?” She grabbed Marla’s arm with girlish glee, shaking it back and forth. “Oh, the power you feel to own one\!”

“Yeah, right before it takes your life.”

“Hey, we all have our kinks.” Something caught Candie’s eye. She smiled brightly, waving her hand in the air excitedly. Marla turned and saw she was waving at Craig and Tweek. “Speaking of kinks. I’m going to go say hi to my new friends.”

Reflex took over. Marla grabbed Candie’s arm and pulled her back.

*Oh no you’re not.*

“Candace, people place boundaries for a reason. Being overly friendly isn’t an excuse to cross them.”

Candie tilted her head and looked at Marla’s grip on her arm. She raised her gaze, flashing a childlike grin.

“Aw, look at how quick you became loyal.” Candie pulled her arm away. “But if you knew what they were saying about them, you wouldn’t be.” Candie winked at Marla and started walking away. “See ya, bestie\!”

Marla scoffed and shook her head as she walked toward her class. It would be hard for Candie to change her mind about Craig and Tweek. Both had shown her kindness in their own way. Her grandmother’s strict, judgmental voice had been completely muted when she put on the new boots that morning. 

They fit perfectly. 

They were the first gift she had ever received that wasn’t something inherited from her mother. The feeling had been strange—fragile and warm, like the happiness she’d only seen in Christmas specials. Something she’d never felt with her grandparents. 

The feeling let her ignore the cramped trailer her dad had taken her to. Ignore the dim lights, the stained walls and threadbare carpet. She had clutched the boots to her chest when she stepped into her small, dark room, stunned and relieved at how quickly they accepted her into their lives. 

*You’re not charity, you’re family.* 

Those words echoed in her head as she fell asleep. They might have just been a simple fact inside of Craig’s head, but to her, it was a lifeline she hadn’t known she needed or even wanted until that moment. 

Despite that, her profile on Craig remained fairly short. His controlled indifference was harder to read than any of the others. The only consistent variable was his devotion to Tweek, though Marla couldn’t see anything romantic in it yet. It seemed more like a habit—a role he performed without emotion, much like his other roles. He fulfilled the social contracts of ‘son’ and ‘big brother’ with clinical precision, but ‘boyfriend’ she wasn’t so sure yet. 

The only possible hint of something between them was the beanie. She’d seen Tweek clutching it in his hands that morning, only for Craig to take it and pull it over his own head with an ease that suggested ownership. It was a stark contrast to the jacket; when she’d returned it the day before, Craig looked at it like she had contaminated it. 

*Which means Tweek is an exception. So far the only one I noticed.*

Her profile on Tweek had grown since yesterday. He never complained or stopped Tricia from hugging him, even though his whole body stiffened every time. He always tried to smile through it, doing his best even when his body seemed to fight him. Tweek listened to her plea for a job with so much attention. Her worries of not being able to master the job quickly was diminished by Tweek's kind words about her. He even gave them free breakfast, his movements and expression asking for nothing in return. A smile that they were able to enjoy things he made himself. He didn’t even want Craig to help clean around the coffeeshop while he was a guest. Tweek carried his burden like a secret, always just wanting to be helpful.  

Tweek was hardworking. 

Kind.

Probably one of the kindest people Marla had met. He was more than his trauma. Though she could also see how the trauma sometimes spoke louder than his gentle nature.

Which was why something still unsettled her. The one thing that she tried to push away. 

Craig’s face when she was describing what triggered Tweek in the K-drama. She didn’t know if Craig and Tweek were together during the accident, so she had spoken carefully, avoiding the word itself and watching his reactions. When she thought she’d uncovered another clue about Tweek’s accident, she had retreated. 

*The guys who assaulted Tweek…they wouldn’t have…God, I hope it's not what I think.* 

All the signs were there. Sexual assault wasn’t something that only happened to the female population. There were episodes in crime dramas where the male victim wouldn't report anything. The low numbers for the male population was not a sign that it didn't happen to them. But she wanted there to be another explanation. She prayed there would be another explanation. 

She’d never prayed before. 

Never had a reason to. 

Maybe Tricia, in her childlike ignorance, had been right. Realizing someone could do something that evil—to Tweek, to anyone—was enough to break your heart. 

*Could it be why Tricia said the adults were being so secretive?* 

In the past two days. she had cycled through more emotions than in her entire life. A small trickle of everything she never felt before. All the things she denied herself because they weren’t necessary. They weren’t needed. Now even if she tried she wouldn't be able to deny herself from feeling. 

She had seen something strange that morning: she could smile. Her face didn’t break from the unfamiliar expression. Smiling came naturally, especially when she was around Tweek. All she wanted to do was offer him one. 

Marla shuffled through the crowd, making her way to the science lab door. She saw Craig and Tweek had stopped a few feet away. Glancing quickly, she caught Tweek’s expression right away—the look of someone betrayed. Before she could peek at them again, she walked through the door. 

*Looks like they’re having a fight. That sucks.* 

Marla would have loved to be able to ask what was wrong, but one thing she couldn’t mute was her grandmother's relentless pursuit in teaching her to mind her own business. There were just too many questions she had, and if people were willing to answer, then what was the harm? Tricia was more than happy to give her as many answers as she could. 

*But maybe asking if they had a fight…yeah, that won’t be good.*

The furniture inside the science lab was surprisingly modern compared to the old windows and walls. The contrast was obvious—six black tabletops surrounded by silver metal stools, with sleek cabinets lining the back wall. The whole room smelled new. Clean. 

Marla found an empty table in the back corner and sat down. The metal stool was cold enough to make her flinch when her knee touched it.

She scanned the room and caught the boy with heterochromia glancing at her. He wore the biggest smug look on his face. 

*Eric Cartman. Ew, no thank you.* 

Marla’s profile on Eric Cartman was short. The few minutes she spent with him that morning was enough to let her know she wanted to stay far away. If manipulation was a person, it would look like Eric. Every alarm in her head had gone off while Butters was trying to introduce them. 

She saw his table was already full. Clyde sat looking down at his phone while talking to the boy with the green hat. The other boy with the blue and red beanie was staring at his phone too. Marla looked away when she noticed Eric leaning onto his arm, staring at her harder.

*Barf. As if\!* 

Tweek walked into the classroom. Without thinking, her hand shot straight up and waved at him. She dropped it quickly, but he had already noticed her. 

As he walked toward the table, a girl wearing a white headband called his name and waved. The girl was pretty in an effortless, understated kind of way. Brown shoulder-length hair framed her face neatly, and there was a calm softness in her brown eyes. 

Tweek waved back before sitting down next to Marla.

*Oh? He has other friends? Or are people just being nice?*

“Hey, you’re in group B,” Tweek said as he took off his backpack and slid it under his seat. 

“Group B?”

“Yeah. Our class size is small so we have two groups. You're either in Group A, which is advanced, or Group B, which is basic.” He crossed his arms on the table and looked at her. “I would have thought you were in advanced.”

“It’s the hair. People assume I’m smart.” She shrugged. “All I need now are glasses to complete the nerd fit.”

Tweek chuckled softly. 

*Why isn’t he stuttering or twitching?* 

“So why is this room brand new?” Marla asked, scooting her stool closer to the table. “My homeroom looked like crap compared to this room.”

“Oh, they shut down the school for renovations one year.” Tweek tilted his head, thinking. “I think they redid this room, added a new wing, and upgraded the cafeteria too.”

“Really? How long ago was that?”

“Seventh grade. No…wait…” His face tightened with frustration. “It had to be eighth grade.” His hand moved to his temple. “Yeah, I think it was a few years ago.”

“You’ve been here so long everything probably just blends together. Don’t worry about it.” She leaned forward slightly. “You know, my high school in New York was built in the nineteen hundreds.” Tweek let his hand fall on the table, looking away. “It looked really old inside and even had some of the original furniture. It kind of felt like being in a museum.”

“T-that… that sounds cool. Are all N-New York schools like that?”

*His stutter came back…memory gap triggers stutter. If that’s the case, then what stopped it earlier?* 

“Maybe.” Marla shrugged. “I just know all the private Catholic schools I went to had that same vibe.” She placed her hands on her lap. “And before you ask, no, I didn’t like going to private schools. The kids were weird.” She looked down at her folder. “They were all fake.”

“I’m s-sorry to hear that.” Tweek’s shoulder twitched. He leaned back and let his hands rest on his lap. “I think in this school all you have to w-worry about is staying away from Cartman.”

“Oh, you guys call him by his last name?” 

Tweek nodded. 

“I like that better,” Marla said. “Don’t worry. I’ll stay far away from that guy.”

The bell rang, and the last few students trickled in, followed by the teacher pushing a grey cart with laptops on top. The teacher was a tall, slender man with a bright face and dark auburn hair. His lab coat was too big for his frame. Like a child playing dress up. The energetic way he moved around the room made it clear he loved teaching. 

*He looks a lot better than that stupid homeroom teacher.*

“So, was Craig always in Group A?” Marla asked, quickly glancing at Tweek and then watching the teacher look through a big stack of papers. “I would’ve never taken Craig as someone in advanced. He looks pretty chill. Not much of a bookworm.” 

The teacher looked up, jerking his head slightly to look toward their table. Marla turned to see what had caught his attention. Tweek was staring intently at an empty space in front of him. 

“Tweek?” 

He wasn’t twitching or moving. He was perfectly still. His eyes stayed open, making tiny, rapid shifts, but he wasn’t blinking. She felt bad for wanting to observe him longer, but it was fascinating. The body and mind work with different agendas. Your body will always be more honest than your mind. The psychological thrillers always said the body never lies. The mind can lie all it wants, but the body will always give it away. Tweek’s body was saying something, and she desperately wanted to translate it.

*Nope. Better make sure he’s ok.*

She leaned over until her shoulder pressed against his arm. She applied pressure slowly, making his body start to lean with her weight. 

“Tweek?” she whispered.

His eyes fluttered, and he turned to look at her. She immediately moved back to her seat. He rubbed his eyes and shook his head, looking disoriented.

“I’m sorry. W-what were we talking about?” he said in a whisper.

*Dissociation. That was definitely dissociation.* 

The teacher came over and crouched down to be eye level with Marla and Tweek. He tapped on the desk gently to get their attention.

“Hey there, guys.” His smile widened. “How’s it going, Tweek?”

“Hello, Mr. J.” Tweek straightened up. 

“You got it.” Mr. J nodded, his voice taking on a delicate tone. “You remembered. Good job.” Tweek hunched over slightly as Mr. J shuffled through his stack and slid Tweek’s paper to him. “Seems Craig’s not with you this year. Hopefully it’s not too bad. Let me know if you need anything.” He then glanced down and pulled out Marla’s paper, lingering on the name at the top. “Oh, are you related to—”

“Yes.”

“That’s great\! We’re happy to have you. Welcome to South Park.” He slid the paper toward her and stood up, walking toward the laptop cart.

*Mr. J: Happy science teacher who doesn’t hate Craig. Noted.* 

“Alright, everyone,” Mr. J raised a paper over his head to get everyone’s attention. “Listen for your laptop numbers. It’s on your page.” He grabbed another stack of paper. “Science contracts. I’ll pass it out after the laptops. Answer the questions and sign it.” 

Marla looked down at her numbers and repeated them a few times to be ready. She turned to look at Tweek. He was biting his nails, staring down at his own numbers. 

“Oh, you don’t have to worry about it.” Mr. J walked over and placed a laptop in front of Tweek. “Here you go.”  

*Way to make it obvious Mr. J.* 

Marla felt a shift in the room. A sudden heaviness and silence. That look that passed over Tweek when the laptop landed on the table. The way his head lowered a little more. His special treatment, front and center. 

*So, they all know. Even the teachers. Which could mean the accident happened at school.*

She propped her chin on her palm as she waited for her number. 

Once she had her laptop, she started to follow the directions on the page. She kept glancing over at Tweek. She saw how he would pause and close his eyes for a few minutes, then go back to work on the laptop. She parted her lips to say something but stopped. 

*Marlene, you’re not a snake. No gossip is good gossip. Don’t ask so many questions.* Her grandmother’s voice was sneaking into her head, but Marla felt her mind filling with questions anyways. She had a full library inside her mind palace, and the shelves were overflowing. 

“You ok?” Tweek was looking at her. “You look like you want to ask me something.”

“Oh, no, no. Just thinking.” 

*Shit, was I making a face? Was I staring?*

“I don't mind.” Tweek turned his body toward her, his shoulder twitching. “I mean…as long as it's not… it’s not a weird private question. Everything else s-should be ok.”

“Ew, no. I’m not Clyde.” Marla scrunched her face at him. “But you shouldn’t give me permission to ask so easily.” She grinned and shook her head. “Seriously, you’re opening Pandora’s box.”

Tweek smiled at her and shrugged. “You’d be the first to ever ask me a-anything. So please, go ahead.”

“What?” 

“Well,” Tweek’s trembling hand started to play with the buttons of his shirt. His eyes scanned the room briefly before he leaned in closer, his voice dropping softly. “I kind of think a lot p-people avoid me…or avoid talking to me.”

“Oh.” 

Marla did a quick glance to her side, then scooted her stool a little closer to him. The girl and the white head band and the boy in the green hat were glancing at them. 

*So they are just friendly but not friends*?

“Why do you think that?” she asked. 

“It’s just s-something I noticed.”  He shook his head. “It might be all in my head.”

“How about this? To make it fair, you get to ask me questions too.” Tweek nodded. “Ok, then I’ll go first.” Marla pointed at Tweek’s forehead where she saw the scar. “That scar and the one on your lip—are both from the accident?” Tweek nodded. “Don’t worry, I won’t ask about the accident—”

“Even if you did, I w-wouldn't be able to tell you much. I don't remember it.”

*He forgot? Well, that makes sense. A trauma that severe, right to the head. Maybe he has memory loss. Oh right. The confession question…he couldn’t answer it.*

“Um… I’m not sure what to ask you.” Tweek pressed his lips together, thinking. “Oh, when is your birthday?” 

“October 22nd.”

“Oh\! One day before Craig’s birthday.”

*Noted, BUT let’s avoid asking about Craig. Memory loss is more interesting.* 

“The scar on your head. Did it make you forget anything?”

Tweek appeared as if he was sinking in his chair. He let his head fall, and his hands clasped together tightly in his lap.

“I lost… I lost three years.” He started to pick at the skin around his thumb. “When I woke up at the hospital, they asked me for my age. I said I was ten.” Tweek’s hands moved, fidgeting with the hem of his shirt. “The doctor said I was actually thirteen. So I f-forgot three whole years of my life.”

*Holy shit, the accident put him in the hospital. Woke up at the hospital? That could suggest there was a major concussion or worse.*

“I’m sorry. If it’s too hard to talk about—”

“No, it’s… it’s fine.” He let his eyes quickly glance at her. “I actually haven’t talked about it before. So it’s fine. I r-really don’t mind.”

*Has no one ever asked him before? It’s been two years\!* 

“Must have been scary…not remembering.” Marla said. She kept her voice low, carefully treading the line between curiosity and intrusion. 

“Yeah, I think I cried for a while.” Tweek’s shoulder twitched. That sharp, involuntary jerk. “And I kept wanting to c-convince them I was ten.” 

*That sounds horrible. Does he still feel like he’s ten? Or does he feel he’s thirteen? I mean he’s fifteen now… NO, wait. I have a better question.* 

“Did you ever get any memories back?”

“A little…I think. My p-parents tried to help with some pictures they had of me. Some m-moments I remembered on my own, but I feel…I feel I’m still missing a lot. No one else has really tried to help me remember.”

*Not even Craig? What's wrong with remembering? But wait…* 

“I wonder why only three years. Usually, people lose a whole lifetime with head traumas or even identities.” Marla crossed her legs and leaned an arm against the table, thinking, flipping through her medical notes inside her head. “You must have reset and gone back to the time you felt the safest. Something happened when you were ten that changed your life for the better.” Tweek’s body went rigid. His eyes widened for a second, then he looked away. “I mean, I could be wrong. I’m not a doctor or anything. I ramble on too, so just ignore me.” Marla’s hands moved to start playing with her skirt. “So, um…I actually don’t remember my mom.” Her fingers fidgeted with the cloth. “At this point, I don’t remember her face or her voice. And, well, my grandparents never talked about her, so…it felt like she never existed.”

*I’m rambling. Shut up\! You’re going to freak him out.* 

Tweek gave her a quick, reassuring smile. 

“I’m s-sorry…I’m sorry to hear a-about your…your m-mom,” he said softly. “Do you miss her?”

*His stuttering…* 

“I can’t, since I don’t remember her.” 

“Oh, right…” Tweek nodded. “It s-sucks when you can’t r-remember. You feel… you feel s-stuck.”

“The blank spaces.” Tweek’s eyes locked on hers for the first time since their conversation started. “The blank spaces don’t let you move on. At least, that’s how I feel about my mom.”

Tweek opened his mouth to say something when he flinched, his hand shooting up to press against his temple. 

“You ok?” Marla leaned closer to him. 

“I just got a weird…sharp pain.” Tweek moved his laptop and leaned forward, laying his head down on his crossed arms. “Sorry,” he said in a muffled voice. 

*Shit, shit, shit. Did I trigger something?*

Mr. J was passing out the science contracts when he noticed Tweek. He walked over and crouched next to Tweek. Marla could hear him whisper something, but Tweek just shook his head in response. Mr. J nodded, his face sympathetic, and left the papers on the table. 

For the rest of the class, Tweek kept his head on his arms. Marla felt incredibly guilty for asking. She wanted to punch herself. She thought if she didn’t talk about the accident specifically, it would be fine. She wished Craig gave her a trigger list so she knew what was safe. 

The bell rang, and Marla closed her laptop. Tweek sat up. There was a red mark on his forehead from where it had rested on his arms, but his messy hair fell over it, hiding it from view. 

“Tweek, I’m sorry for asking you—”

“I said it was ok to ask.” Tweek rubbed his head. “I guess this is why people… people avoid talking to me.”

*Oh Tweek…*

He grabbed his backpack and then slowly stood up, his hand gripping the edge of the table for balance. She wanted to reach over and help him, but remembering the defeated expression he’d given Mr. J, she decided against it. He opened his backpack and tried to place the laptop in with trembling hands. 

“Oh, I can carry your laptop too,” Tweek offered. “We’re going to the same class anyway.”

“Thank you.” 

Something inside was telling her to let him carry it. Like refusing might make him feel worse. He smiled as he grabbed her laptop, rolling his shoulders back. He carried the backpack like it weighed nothing. Tweek grabbed the stack of signed science contracts left behind from a few nearby tables and handed them to Mr. J. With a nod and a wave, they made their way out into the hall. 

The loud voices crashed into them. Marla caught sight of Craig walking down the hall toward them. Her chest tightened when she saw Candie standing right next to him. 

*Well, shit.* 

Candie’s mouth didn't stop moving. She gazed up at Craig with round, curious eyes. Craig’s expression was the same as always—deadpan and unimpressed. But his eyes darkened as they fell on Marla and Tweek. 

Before she could react, Tweek turned and walked the other way. She stood there for a second, shocked by his sudden retreat. Back in Tricia’s room, during the short moment Craig was there, Tweek had looked more relaxed. His twitches barely visible. She had filed that data away and noticed it again in the morning.
