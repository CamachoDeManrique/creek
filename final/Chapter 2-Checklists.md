**Chapter 2**

**Craig- Checklists**

As soon as Craig reached Tweek’s bed, his whole body gave up. The mattress swallowed him, cool sheets clinging to his skin. He stretched out, a full-body sigh leaving him. His eyelids were heavy, fighting a losing battle. Before they were completely shut, he pulled out his phone and texted his mom he was going to stay over. It was too late— Tweek’s house was closer, he was too tired to keep walking. The phone clacked against the nightstand. He lay back, took a deep breath, and hated how even at Tweek’s house, it still smelled like coffee. It was a smell you could get tired of easily. He let his arm rest over his eyes. His shirt sleeve smelled like caramel and coffee—some spilled drink that splashed on him. He scoffed and let it fall back to his side. 

He hated working the front counter at Tweak’s Coffeehouse. Talking to people was exhausting enough; pretending to be polite was worse. Every day, someone found a new way to impress him with their stupidity and lack of common sense. Today a man locked himself in the restroom. Instead of following Craig’s instructions, he started pounding on the door like a lunatic, shoving at it with his shoulder and back. Panic attack, apparently. Craig understood those, but this one was over-the-top ridiculous. The kind of dramatic panic attacks in TV shows or movies. Before he could decide whether the guy was truly faking it, Mr. Tweak came and opened the door with a key. The man demanded a free coffee for “the trauma”. Craig hated when people threw that word around like nothing. Trauma wasn’t a coupon for a free coffee. Mr. Tweak, of course, gave in. Craig made it decaf. Anyone who annoyed him got decaf. As the man snatched the coffee cup from Craig and walked out, Craig silently wished he tripped on his shoelace and face-planted on the floor— or better yet, that the coffee gave him violent diarrhea. 

Then there was the woman. A tall, influencer type that walked in and ordered a *deconstructed* latte. She had bright red hair and an annoying voice to match. Craig served her three separate cups— milk, espresso, and sugar. She filmed a Tik Tok, combining them all together on the counter. When she finished, she looked up from her phone long enough to ask for his number. He declined and told her he was sixteen. She pouted, left her trash, and walked out. Was she mad because he declined or because he was sixteen? How was that any of his fault? 

Adults were so useless. They were good at disappointing him all the time. But he’d rather deal with people than have Tweek deal with them. He was glad his parents had let him work at Tweak’s Coffeehouse for the last two summers. Mr. Tweak let Craig work there on the condition that he handle the front counter and all the customers. Mr. Tweak didn’t like dealing with people, but he didn’t have the excuse that Tweek did. Tweek’s anxiety didn’t let him manage the front— he worked drinks and baking instead. 

As Craig began to blink into sleep, he hoped Tweek had enjoyed his day off with Tricia. Tweek never got days off unless it was for school— it was annoying. Not to Tweek, but to Craig. Tweek always said he’d rather be doing something rather than nothing, so he didn’t need time off. Craig knew Tweek just didn’t want to be alone with his thoughts. 

He glanced lazily around the room. Model replicas of airplanes and Lego landmarks lined the shelves against Tweek’s wall. Tweek was good at learning things quickly— too quickly— and then getting bored, moving on to the next thing to learn. In the end, Craig had suggested that Tweek hang out with Tricia on his days off. They used to do that all the time before the accident. Tweek wouldn’t remember that, but Tricia and Craig did. That’s all they had left of the old Tweek. 

He shook his head slightly on the pillow, pushing the thoughts away.

Hanging out with Tricia worked for a while, until something always came up and Mr. Tweak pulled Tweek back to the coffeehouse. Just like today, Mr. Tweak had asked Tweek to run an errand— something Craig could’ve done himself, if he’d been asked. It wasn’t as busy by then. 

Frustrating.

But. . . Tweek had actually seemed relieved to see Craig when he walked in. He almost looked happy. Tweek faked happiness for other people, but this looked real. For a second, Craig’s breath caught in his throat when he saw it— the expression he’d almost forgotten existed. Why was he so happy to see Craig?

Before Craig could sit up and think about it, his thoughts flickered out like a candle flame. And he was asleep.

A slow blink— his eyes opened to darkness. 

Craig blinked a few times to let his eyes adjust. The room was dimly washed in silver light slipping through the curtains. Something was wrong. A weight pressed against his chest, his muscles were tense, the kind of tension that felt left over from something unseen.

From something forgotten.

He could never remember his dreams. Sleep was always one long blink, and then it was morning. But the feelings lingered— a haunting of whatever he’d dreamed. He tried to move his head a little but it felt heavy. 

*Maybe it was a nightmare.*

He shut his eyes again, but a faint murmur broke through the silence. Craig’s eyes shot open and he turned his head quickly and froze.

Tweek was beside him.

Craig leaned over, switching on the lamp on the nightstand. The soft yellow light revealed Tweek trembling harder than usual. His arms wrapped tightly around his knees as he rocked back and forth. Craig sat up, paying close attention. He heard low, uneven mumbles beneath his breath. Then a quiet sniffle. A trembling hand desperately wiping at tears.

Craig reached out and set his hand slowly on Tweek’s shoulder.

Tweek jolted, his eyes snapping open— wide, startled, like he’d forgotten Craig was ever there. 

“Babe,” Craig said quietly. “Is something wrong?. . .Was it a nightmare?”

Tweek shook his head quickly, shrugging Craig’s hand off. Craig let it fall on the bed. 

*Okay. No touching.*

Craig took a deep breath and looked closer at Tweek. He wasn’t in his pajamas. He didn’t even take off his shoes. His hair looked damp. He had felt warm to the touch. Putting all of that together, Craig realized Tweek was in the middle of a panic attack. 

He rose slowly and knelt beside the bed, careful to give Tweek space. Being too close during one of these episodes never helped— it made it worse. And sometimes, when Tweek fell too deep into the rabbit hole, the panic turned to anger. The spiral came fast— thoughts folding in on themselves, twisting into hopelessness, then fury. Craig learned that the hard way. 

“I— just couldn’t sleep,” Tweek said softly, wiping at his face. “My parents, they— I keep thinking . . . And my head hurts . . .” his breath was starting to come in faster. “I don't know . . . I want to go to sleep, but I can’t . . . I didn’t want to wake you up. I’m sorry I woke you.” Tweek buried his head between his knees. “I'm sorry . . . I'm sorry.”

*Why is he having a panic attack?* 

Craig started to go over everything that happened during the day.

*No. Stop. Focus.* 

He sat on the edge of the bed and thought about what he needed to say.

*Make them feel safe. Keep your voice even.*

“Tweek,” Craig leaned over a little so he didn’t need to speak loudly. “You’re okay. You’re in your room. You’re safe.”

Tweek shook his head. His legs fell limply, chest rising too fast, breath sharp and uneven. His hands gripping at his shirt tightly and eyes locked on a spot on the bed.

Craig was there when Mr. Tweak was talking to him briefly about his plans for the future. He was surprised that it triggered Tweek. All they wanted to know was if Tweek wanted to go to college or take over the coffeehouse. Craig didn’t see any change to Tweek during the conversation. Besides the normal twitch and hand tremors. Since everything looked fine Craig clocked out and walked to Tweeks house to rest like he always did. Was there something else that happened after he left?

*Stop. Remind them to breathe.*

“I need you to look at me.” Craig leaned to the side to get into Tweeks line of vision. 

“No\!” Tweek scrambled off the bed, his legs brushing against Craig. 

He started pacing— one way to the door and to his dresser. Craig stood slowly, careful not to startle him further.

“Tweek, look at me please.” he said again— calm, steady. Tweek’s eyes drifted to Craig’s. “Let’s breathe together. You’re going down a rabbit hole.”

Tweek covered his face. If he didn’t stop him soon, Tweek would either pass out or start to throw things— it always went one of two ways.

*Stay calm.*

“Dude, let’s just take a minute—” Craig tried again.

Tweek let out a groan. “They asked about us. . . our future plans. . if we stay together. . . ” His hands fell to his shirt again, gripping at it. “Why wouldn’t we stay together? We have to stay together, right?”

*Oh. There it is.*

This was a new development. Craig noticed it got worse this summer. Mr. Tweak really liked boasting about having a gay son at the coffeehouse during tourist season. He’d gotten a lot of money before from naive tourists who wanted to help the “poor gay kids existing in a small mountain town”. A tourist played twenty questions with them while they were working together. Craig realized it had been a long time since they have talked about their relationship. He decided to completely ignore the questions; he’d rather not talk about those things with a stranger. He had looked at Tweek who was just twitching and repeating he didn’t remember. 

A few hours later he found Tweek having a panic attack in the storage room. Craig thought the unwanted attention was the trigger but Tweek’s ramblings were about them. A lot of broken sentences and anxiety. Craig never understood why their relationship became a trigger, when he tried to ask, Tweek just shut down. They weren’t a perfect couple, but they understood each other really well before the accident happened. 

Tweek couldn’t remember what happened to him the day of the accident. Parts of his life were suddenly missing. The doctor said it was like someone took a sharp pencil and started poking holes in Tweeks memories. Three years of being together faded into nothing after the accident. You could never be sure what he could and couldn’t remember. You couldn’t ask either. Tweek changed. He stopped talking and just started twitching more and more. The life that Craig remembered before that day seemed to blur more and more as time passed. Never really going away but haunting the rooms in his mind. 

Craig pinched the bridge of his nose, pushing away the memory, swallowing the bitter feeling crawling its way up his throat. He wished he had his own reset button. He wanted the luxury of forgetting.

“Babe, today is Sunday.” Craig raised his hands in front of him and took one step towards Tweek. “You’re in your room. Nothing will happen to you here.” He took another step. “You need to breathe. Forget everything else.” His hands were only inches away. “I need you to breathe.” 

Tweek stopped pacing and looked at him. He swayed a little and fell back on the dresser. Craig's hands held on to his shoulder, keeping him up. No flinch. No reaction.

*Good.*

Tweek's stiff shoulders felt hot and damp beneath Craig’s cold hands. “Okay, do what I do. Remember, five seconds inhale, and five seconds exhale.” Craig counted under his breath as Tweek started to inhale. “One...two…three…four…five.”

With four sets of breathing Tweek’s shoulders loosened and slumped. Tweek’s face glistened with sweat. 

*Ground them back to the present.*

“Tweek, I need you to answer my questions.” Craig looked around the room. The yellow light wasn’t bright enough for him to ask Tweek to look for a certain object. He looked back at Tweek, who was looking up at him. “What color is my hair?”

“Black.”

“What color is your shirt?"

“....Green.”

“Good.” Craig sighed and let go of Tweek’s shoulders. “Can you remember anything else in the room that's green?”

“...your eyes.”

“They’re not green. They’re brown.”

Tweek blinked, confused, and leaned in a little closer— studying Craig’s irises in the low light.

“What?. . No…wait…really?”

Craig let the corner of his mouth curl upward. “I’m kidding.” He lifted his hand and gently pushed Tweek’s face away with his palm. “Dork.”

Tweek exhaled a small laugh and shoved Craig’s shoulder.

*Good. He’s coming back.*

He let himself fall back on the edge of the bed. Craig sighed with relief. Small victory in an ongoing war. The room began to feel lighter again, the air moving freely. During Tweek’s panic attacks, Craig always felt so focused the walls seemed to close in on them. 

Tweek hesitated, then sat beside him. He pressed his lips tightly together and looked away from Craig, embarrassed. The awkward silence that followed always annoyed Craig. It’s not Tweek's fault he is the way he is. Why feel embarrassed about needing help? Of course, no matter how many times he’d say that, Tweek would still act the same way.

Craig leaned back on his arms and closed his eyes. Sometimes he could almost hear all of Tweek's thoughts buzzing inside of that head of his. The constant loop of worrying and over thinking. Tweek was trying so hard to keep it all inside. 

Craig remembered complaining about Tweek’s constant need to tell him everything when they were kids. The way he twitched, yelped, and shouted whenever something made him anxious. Craig regretted ever trying to escape that. He’d give anything to have the old Tweek back— the one who couldn’t keep quiet, who didn’t swallow everything until it broke him. The one that wanted to share everything with Craig. 

He opened his eyes and turned to look at Tweek.

*It's not his fault.*

“I’m sorry…” Tweek broke the silence.

Right on cue. The apology that wasn’t necessary.

“It’s okay. You know I'm here to help.” Craig sat up and turned his body toward him. “Did something happen at my house?”

“Hmmmm. . . no . . . nothing.” Tweek didn’t meet his eyes. “It must have just… been— been a long day for me.”

*Liar.*

“Really?” Craig kept his tone calm, his gaze fixed on Tweek. “Nothing?”

“Well . . . I just watched another drama with Tricia.” Tweek's fingers fidgeted on his lap, twisting together at the ends of his shirt. “Oh and Marla. She’s— she’s nice. She asks a lot of questions but— but I think she’s nice.”

“Who’s Marla?”

Tweek smiled weakly and playfully shoved Craig’s arm.

“She’s your cousin, you loser. The girl we met today.” Craig nodded and shrugged. He barely remembered her. “You’re both kind of alike.” Tweek looked down at his hands. “I think you’d get along with Marla. She doesn’t like Korean dramas either.”

“They’re really predictable.” Craig muttered. "I don’t get why you and Tricia like them so much.”

“They’re not all bad. Although, Tricia does mostly watch love stories.” Tweek stayed quiet for a moment then started to fidget and play with the bed covers. “Marla did ask me something. . .I felt dumb because I didn't know the answer.”

“What?”

“How did we start dating?”

Craig blinked a few times. 

“I don't know if I should tell you.” he said as he leaned forward. “Our relationship is a—”

“I don’t want it to be another trigger.” Tweek said quickly. “I really want to know.”

Craig fiddled with the last button on his shirt, unsure if he should tell him. He cleared his throat. 

“I don’t know dude. I think we’ll be pushing it.”

Tweek's shoulders slumped. 

“I just— I just hate that I can't remember . . . ” Tweek said as he bowed his head. “It feels . . . like I’m being left out . . . and I feel really empty." Tweek let his head fall into his hands. “I should be fine. Can you please tell me?”

*Damn it.*

Craig saw how Tweek gripped at the bottom of his shirt. Tweek’s shirts had permanent wrinkles from all the times he grips at them. 

*Maybe the past won’t trigger anything.*

“Okay.” Craig nodded and moved a little closer to Tweek. “I’ll tell you.”

Tweek sat up straight holding his hands on his lap. He was already fidgeting.

“Well, you came to my house and you were dressed in a suit,” Craig said as he reached over and gently pinched Tweek’s fidgeting hand. “And you had this big poster board that had ‘Will you be my boyfriend?’ written on it.”

Tweek’s eyes widened and he playfully pushed Craig.

“You’re lying\! Oh man, I would have died\!” Tweek smiled. “I would never do that\! That’s too much pressure\!” Craig smiled and then looked down. “Dude, tell me the truth.”

“Okay. Okay.” Craig sighed. “The truth is, some asian girls were drawing us together as a couple.”

“Why were they doing that?”

“I don't know.” Craig shrugged and leaned his elbows on his legs. “But everyone seemed to think we were dating. The first gay kid couple in South Park. The adults became weirdly obsessed with us.”

Tweek looked down. “Oh my god. . . oh my god\!” Tweek raised his hands to the side of his head and grabbed his hair with a smile on his face. “I remember. . .Oh god. . . the drawings\!” He covered his face. “They were so cringey\!”

Craig smiled at the blush taking over Tweek’s face.

“Yeah, and some were even framed and hung up in people’s houses.” Craig said. “I think my dad collected some too.”

“Aaarrgh\!” Tweek said as a grunt escaped him. He fell back on the bed. “That’s so embarrassing\! No wonder I forgot\!” He moved his hands away and sat up. “Wait… we broke up though, right? I kind of remember yelling at you about cheating or something.”

“Huh, your memory is really kicking in.” Craig nodded. “Yeah, you really made me into the bad guy. The whole school hated me for a while.”

Tweek’s eyes were looking around in front of him as if he was turning pages in his head to find the memories.

“Right.” His eyes stopped. “We wanted them to stop drawing us.” He looked up at Craig’s face. “But how did we get back together?”

“Well, you came to my house with a suit on and a big poster board—” Tweek pushed Craig off the bed before he could finish.

“No I didn’t\!”

“How are you sure? Maybe you don't remember that part.”

“Dude, seriously.” Tweek leaned over and grabbed a pillow to throw at Craig. “You suck.”

Craig dodged the pillow as he sat on the floor and leaned on the bed, looking up at Tweek. He let out a long tired sigh. 

“You know…” Tweek spoke softly as he looked down at his hands. “The only thing I remembered when I woke up. . . that time. . . was that you were my boyfriend…” Tweeks' eyes wandered towards the ceiling like his memories were now floating above him. 

Craig lifted his arm over the bed and rested his chin on it. Tweek never talked about the day he woke up from the accident. He felt happy to hear Tweek share something. Craig could listen to him talk forever now.

Tweek bit at his lip and his shoulder started to twitch.

“I remembered. . . . we had to stay together… no matter what. . . because a lot of people would be sad if we didn't.” He looked down at Craig. “Our feelings didn’t matter and it didn’t matter that we aren't gay. I just—” Tweek started to bite at his nails. “I just remember feeling that. . . you’re a really good friend . . . and this is all pretend.”

Those last words that fell so easily from Tweek's lips hit Craig hard against the chest. He could only respond by slowly nodding and looking away.

*If I told you, you wouldn’t believe me.*

Tweek dropped his hand and leaned over.

“I forgot something, didn't I?” Tweek asked. Craig turned and looked up at him. “You look like— like I forgot something important about us.”

“You mean the poster board?” Craig smirked.

“That didn’t happen\!” Tweek threw another pillow.

“You don't know that.” Craig caught it and threw it back. “Your memory sucks.”

It was better to just leave things as they were. He didn’t have the strength to fix Tweek again. After the accident, Craig was so focused on getting Tweek back to some type of normal, that everything else took a back seat. There were other things that needed immediate attention. And now, he wasn’t so sure what he felt. 

The sleepiness that had him pinned only moments ago was coming back. Craig stretched out his arms and leaned his head against the bed. He let out a loud yawn and started to unbutton his shirt. He wanted to change into his pajamas, his undershirt felt gross and heavy. Mr. Tweak insisted he wear a uniform: black long sleeve button shirt and black pants. He hated how uncomfortable he felt in them after his shift. He could care less how aesthetically pleasing it was for work. 

He stood up to walk to the closet when Tweek grabbed his wrist and pulled him back. Craig turned and saw Tweek frozen, eyes wide. Mouth partly open. His whole body looked stiffened like a statue. Was he remembering something else?

“Tweek?”

There was a long pause and then Tweek blinked and looked up at Craig. He looked down at his hand and quickly let go of Craig’s wrist. 

“I’m sorry.” Tweek said as he sat on his knees. The bed covers shuffling underneath him “I didn’t mean to— to grab you. . . .I don't know why I did that.”

Craig sat on the edge of the bed.

“Hey, it’s ok.” Craig said softly. “Let’s not go down another rabbit hole tonight.” Tweek nodded and pressed his lips tightly together. “Just breathe and I’m going to get up, and I’m going to get the sleeping bag and change into pajamas, ok?”

Tweek nodded. As Craig tried to get up Tweek reached out and grabbed his arm to hold him back. Tweek’s bright blue eyes full of tears waiting for their cue to fall.

“I...  I don't know why I keep doing that.” Tweek’s voice cracked. “I don’t know what's happening.” The tears finally broke free and fell down Tweek’s face. 

Craig sat back down. 

It was his fault. He shouldn't have answered his question. It felt nice to finally talk about them but he let it go on for too long. 

“It’s ok.”  Craig said calmly. “We’ll just sit here until you’re ready.”

Tweek nodded and let go of Craig. He moved all the way to the edge of the bed to sit against the wall, taking deep breaths. 

*Good. At least he can do his breathing exercises*.

After a few minutes, Tweek was back again. He started to fidget with the bed covers and his shoes. He kept biting his lower lip and glancing at Craig. Craig’s eyes kept falling shut; he had to force himself to keep them open. He had leaned against the headboard waiting for Tweek to calm down, and the sandman wasn’t letting him off easy. His head felt heavy and he wanted to close his eyes. He didn’t want to wait anymore for Tweek to ask what he wanted.

“Dude,” Tweek jumped at the sound of Craig’s voice. “Would you mind if I just slept on the bed?”

“Oh\!” Tweek looked relieved. “I don’t mind at all.”

Craig knew that was what Tweek wanted to ask but couldn’t bring himself to. The panic attack left him feeling unsteady so Craig being as close as possible always helped. Of course, Tweek would never admit that; he’d just bite his lips or his nails because he didn't want to bother Craig. He’d gotten pretty good at figuring out what Tweek needs, he just wished he didn’t have to.

Tweek moved off the bed and took off his shoes. Craig pulled the covers away.

“Oh, wait.” Tweek grabbed the cover and looked at the bed. “Do you think we’ll fit?”

“Yeah,” Craig said as he dropped himself on the bed and laid on his right side, making sure he left enough room between him and the wall for Tweek to fit in the middle. “I sleep on my side anyways.”

“You don't sleep on your side.” Tweek said as crawled on the bed staying closer to the wall.

“You watch me sleep?” Craig yawned and closed his eyes. “Gross, stalker.”

“I don't watch you sleep.” Tweek pulled the covers over them. “And we’re dating, so I can’t be your stalker.”

“Oh, speaking of dating.” Craig opened his eyes and got the energy to reach for his phone on the nightstand. “Want to see my new wallpaper?”

He unlocked his phone and showed Tweek the picture he took of him with the pigtails and surprised look on his face. Tweek squinted his eyes and pouted.

“I knew you were lying.” Tweek said as he tried to take the phone; Craig lifted it over his head. “Dude, I hate you.”

“You wish you did.” Craig said as he looked at his phone and noticed his mom sent him a message. He swiped it open.

**Goodnight. Please come home tomorrow before 8AM. I need you to take care of Tricia.**

Craig let out a small grunt. He set his alarm and placed the phone back on the nightstand. 

“You know, you *could* stop taking embarrassing pictures of me.” Tweek said as he let out a loud yawn. 

“Tomorrow honey, I have an early meeting in the morning.” Craig said as he closed his eyes. He felt Tweek poke him in the ribs. “Ow.”

“Did— did you ever tell me why you take embarrassing pics of me?” Tweek asked softly. His voice sounded groggy and slower.

“Well, I’m not staring at it if that’s what you’re worried about.” Craig yawned so hard his jaw hurt. He opened his eyes to pinch Tweek's nose. “Geez, get over yourself.” He muttered. Another poke to the ribs. “Ow.” Craig couldn’t keep his eyes open anymore. “Go to sleep Tweek. I’ll tell you tomorrow.”

Craig felt a shift next to him, and then Tweek’s hair was tickling the bottom of his chin. He didn’t have the strength anymore for anything. He could feel his strength drain slowly from his body. A memory made its way out.

They were ten years old. Tweek was in Tricia’s room. She had painted his nails and was trying to do his makeup— bright pink cheeks and uneven purple glitter eyeshadow. Craig had sneaked in and took a picture. When he looked at it, he laughed so hard Tweek and Tricia noticed him. They both started to gang up on him to take away the phone. Craig couldn’t stop laughing. He’d never laughed that hard in his life. 

He never laughed that way ever again.

*Yeah, that’s how it started.*

Craig’s phone vibrated. His eyes shot open. The sun was sneaking in through the curtains. It was already morning. Tweek wasn’t next to him. He groaned and moved to grab his phone.

He sat up and answered.

“Hi, mom.”

“Good morning, Craig. I didn’t know if you saw my message.”

“I saw it.” Craig stood up and started walking toward the restroom. “I’m on my way home.”

“Ok, great.” he heard his mom sigh, “you know, it wouldn’t hurt to at least acknowledge that you read my message.”

“You have that feature on your phone that shows I read it, remember?”

“Well, still. It wouldn’t hurt to reply back.”

Craig stepped into the restroom and turned on the light. He put his mom on speakerphone and set it down on the counter.

“You know I suck at little details.”

“Just the ones you don't think are important.” she said, Craig paused, then gave a half-shrug. He squeezed toothpaste onto his dark blue toothbrush and turned on the sink. “So, you’re on your way home? Sounds like you’re in the restroom.”

“I’m getting there,” he said with his toothbrush against his teeth. “Don’t want morning breath.” 

“Well, you haven’t let me down yet.” she said, sighing again. It wasn’t an annoyed sigh— it was a kind of giving-in sigh. He started to count in his head to make sure he brushed for the full two minutes. “Thank you for cleaning the house before you went to work, by the way.”

“Mhmm.”

“If you can eat breakfast at Tweek’s, please try. We’re out of groceries until your dad and I get paid on Friday.” Craig spit, rinsed his mouth, and wiped his face with a hand towel. “If Manuela is there, please let her know I said hi and thank you.”

“They all went to work,” he said. “The house is quiet.”

“Okay, well— don’t forget to thank them for me.”

“Yeah.” He took the phone off speaker and held it back to his ear. “I’ve got some money left. I can pick up a few things.”

“Oh no, Craig, I could never ask you to do that. You earned that money. Use it for yourself. It’s our job to provide for you guys, not the other way around.”

Craig rolled his eyes. The guilt of existing and being a burden was loud when there wasn’t any money. He’d rather they accept his help, then make the guilt heavier on his shoulders. He never understood where the money went. It just evaporated. Both his parents had jobs but seemed to always be struggling. Ever since he started working he’d read all he could on budgeting and saving. He wanted to make sure his money didn’t disappear like a family curse. 

“Okay, mom,” he said. “Whatever you say.”

“Don’t worry too much about it. School starts tomorrow, so you both get free breakfast and lunch anyways.”

Craig brushed his hair with Tweek’s comb and turned off the light before heading downstairs. He had left his shoes by the front door last night— he’d stepped in mud from the night before walking back through the park.

“Mom, I’m walking home now.” he said as he balanced his way into his shoes. 

“Okay\! Be safe.”

He hung up. 

He stepped out and locked the door behind him. Shoving the spare key in his back pocket. The cold air was trying to sneak its way under his clothes. He started to button his shirt back up as he walked down the sidewalk. Wearing the same clothes as yesterday clung to him like damp fabric. His skin didn’t fit right. 

The neighborhood was quiet. The air felt still— everyone had already disappeared into their Monday routines.

His phone vibrated.

1 new message: Clyde

Craig ignored it. 

His alarm went off as he was putting his phone away— he shut it off and his wallpaper appeared. He looked at Tweek’s picture. He hoped the morning was kinder to him than yesterday. He stuffed his phone back in his pocket and pushed the thoughts away.

When he got home, Tricia ran up from the kitchen with open arms. Craig stopped her from hugging him with a hand to her forehead.

“Nope. I need to shower first.” He said as she swatted at his hand.

“Ew, why?”  

“I fell asleep in these clothes. I’m gross.” 

“That doesn’t sound gross,” said a voice from the couch. 

Craig looked over. The girl had her legs crossed and was leaning on her arm looking at them. 

*Marla. Right.* 

“Maybe to you. But for me, it's uncomfortable,” he said as he walked past her towards the stairs. “And gross.”  

“Hey,” said Marla. “Was Tweek ok yesterday?”

Craig stopped and turned to look at her. He stepped down from the first step he took.

“Why?” Craig’s voice sharpened just enough as he turned back.

“Well, the drama sort of set him off.” 

“What?”

He turned and looked at Tricia. She was fiddling with the strings of her hoodie and shifting from one foot to the other.

“Uh…well, he kinda had a tiny panic attack,” Tricia said hesitantly. “But it was super tiny.” She used her fingers to show a small space between her thumb and index finger. “Like this tiny.”

“Why would he panic?” Craig asked as he tilted his head.

Tricia hunched over a little and dropped her hands.

“I don't know...” Tricia shrugged and shoved her hands in her hoodie. “I didn’t see any of the stuff you told me to watch for, so I don’t get why he freaked out.”

He couldn’t get mad at her. He learned the hard way what set off Tweek. Maybe there was a new one he didn’t know about.
