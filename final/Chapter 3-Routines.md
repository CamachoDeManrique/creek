**Chapter 3**

**Tweek- Routines**

Tweek drifted on calm waters— gently swaying. A rare stillness settling in. A sense of peace. Safety. He looked at the starry sky washed in different shades of blue and bleeding purples. The stars were scattered everywhere like silver glitter. The water held him tight and pushed him upward, keeping him from sinking too low. A world made only of sky and water— no buildings, no people, no pain, just blue stretching forever.

“*Tweek, wake up*.”

A calm, deep voice echoed from the distance— almost a whisper. A tiny ripple across the calm surface. Tweek turned his head toward the voice. There was no horizon, no shore, just sky melting into water.

The voice pulled at him— recognizable, but too distant to name.

He wanted to get closer.

“*Babe*.”

The voice came again— soft and gentle. Another ripple coming towards him. Tweek stood on top of the water. He could see the ripple brush against his feet, gentle and warm.

*Babe? Someone calls me babe?*

He tried to remember.

A new feeling crept into him— like a giant spider crawling across his chest. Something odd and foreign. 

He should remember. It was something that should have never been forgotten. 

Tweek wanted to move and hurry towards the voice. Whoever it was, they knew everything. A sinking feeling settled in his stomach. There was more to that voice. 

*They need something from me. . .*

Tweek tried to take a step, but his legs wouldn't move. Frozen. Numb. He looked down and saw the water coiled tightly around his ankle. 

***Stay.***

A small, frightened voice whispered from somewhere in the sky. He looked up.

***Don’t go.***

Tweek reached up towards the stars. His fingertips felt the warmth of their fire as he pulled them away. 

*Is there a kid there?*

A hand suddenly touched his shoulder, shaking him gently. He turned, no one was there. 

“*It’s time to wake up*.”

There was a patience and sweetness he could feel from the voice. He placed his hand on his shoulder where he felt the touch.

*I know you.*

A clean click echoed across the water, and the stars blinked out. The sky went white-blue in an instant, too bright to be real. Tweek looked up.

*Craig.*

 

His eyes opened slowly. The dream melted away, erasing itself as he tried to hold onto it. 

His vision swam in blurs of light and color. He blinked, waiting for the world to settle. His eyelids felt unbearably heavy, his whole body dragging against gravity. There was an unseen pull inside his mind to go back to sleep. To try and go back to wherever he was. 

Tweek turned and rubbed his eyes with the back of his hands, forcing himself upright. There would be no going back. No matter how much he slept, it was never enough. His body always wanted more.

A lost cause. 

He stretched out his arms, surprised he’d managed to sleep for so long. He didn’t remember waking up once. He usually tossed and turned and woke up 6-8 times. His hands felt cold and stiff. He rubbed his palms together. The room was completely still. Something tugged at the back of his mind. Something he’d forgotten.

A soft squeak broke the silence. 

He turned automatically to look at the habitat. 

*Oh yeah, we built it yesterday. Craig had to sleep over.*

After they had come back from the store last night, they hauled all the supplies to his room. Their summer project finally complete. The habitat for Stripe fit perfectly on top of his bigger dresser. But something happened after they set up the last piece of the habitat.

*What was it?* 

The box. 

Stripe had escaped the small box they placed him in. 

Tweek’s neck twitched. A spark of unease shot down his spine.

*Craig said he’d find him. Stripe always came back.*

Tweek gripped his hands as the tremors started. His shoulder jerked. 

*Maybe he got out. Maybe the squeak was just in my dreams. What if Stripe hurts himself again?*

From the corner of his eye, he noticed Craig sitting at the edge of the bed, staring at him. Tweek jumped a little; he didn’t notice he was there. Tweek looked down and hugged himself to stop the tremors. Craig slowly placed a hand over Tweek’s leg.

“Dude, you ok?” Craig asked.

“Y-yes, I think so,” Tweek said as he cleared his throat. Craig gave his leg a small, quick squeeze before pulling his hand back. “Uh. . . did you sleep ok?”

Tweek looked up at Craig. He looked tired— really tired. It was in his eyes. Even in the morning light, the green was muted. A sad, washed-out color. Muted green, like Mr. Tucker had sometimes. 

“Hmmm. . . not really,” Craig smirked. “You hogged all the blankets and kept kicking me off.”

*I did?. . . wait. . .*

Craig tilted his head. The green in his eyes shifted.

*Oh, he’s joking.*

Tweek rolled his eyes and shook his head.

Craig’s face didn’t show much— his expression was always the same. But sometimes Tweek could read him through his eyes. There was a way that the color of his eyes shifted like magic. It was a curious thing. Tweek was sure it was just a trick of the light, the way Craig moves his head when he is angry or sad. Or like now, with that slight tilt that made his eyes gleam a soft green, like new leaves on a tree. The color that says he is having fun.

*Wait— I tried to kick him off the bed?*

“You. . . you slept on my bed?” asked Tweek.

“Well, there wasn’t any space to set the sleeping bag.” Craig glanced at the piles of trash around the room. “I wanted to clean, but it was so late I just crashed.” He rubbed the back of his neck. “I’m sorry I didn’t ask. I just didn't want to wake you—”

“Oh\! It’s. . . it’s ok.” Tweek waved his hands in front of him quickly. “I don’t mind. Really. I just hope that I left— I left you enough room.”

Craig ruffled Tweek’s hair.

“Yeah, don’t worry.”

A squeak made Tweek jump.

*Oh right. Stripe\!*

“You found Stripe?” Tweek said as his shoulder and neck twitched. “He’s ok?”

“Yeah, he’s fine. Just waiting for breakfast.” Craig stood. “I got your clothes ready by the way.” He gestured toward Tweek’s other dresser as he took out his phone. “Try to remember to do the same thing tonight.” 

“Yup, I know.”

“And don’t forget to wash your face first.”

Tweek nodded. 

*Yeah, I remember. Wash my face first.* 

Tweek shuffled to the edge of the bed, letting his feet dangle for a few seconds. He gripped the covers and took a deep breath.

He stood up slowly and made sure the room wasn’t spinning before taking the first step. He’d made the mistake many times before of jumping out of bed and then landing on his face from the blood rushing to his head. He never used to have an issue getting up quickly before, but after the accident, he noticed he had to always stand up slowly. 

Taking a few seconds to take a deep breath was part of the morning routine Craig helped him create. They had written out easy to remember routines last summer. Craig read somewhere that having things written down helped to trigger a memory. Tweek only needed to glance at the paper that had his morning and night routine once or twice. By now, he felt pretty confident, even when Craig wasn’t around.

*Did I have a routine before the accident?* 

There was a blank space where the information should be. He scratched his head as he walked to the bathroom.

*I’m sure I did. What was the first thing I did when I woke up?* 

He turned on the sink. Again, nothing came to mind. A smell resurfaced. 

*Oh coffee.* 

He nodded. He could see the cup of coffee he would have every morning. 

*I wonder if I can have some before we go.*

He splashed water over his face. He kept his head down to let the water droplets fall. He watched as the water swirled and went down the drain.

*Wait. Go where? Why are we getting ready?*

 

Tweek blinked—

— and he was back in his room.

His eyes wandered— then froze on Craig standing in front of him, buttoning the green long-sleeve shirt Tweek was suddenly wearing. Tweek jumped.

*Why is he dressing me? Did I take off my pajamas?*

Tweek hated his mind blanking out— slipping into autopilot and suddenly appearing somewhere. Like an unwanted teleportation trick. One second in pajamas, the next in the hallway, ready and dressed, with no memory of the steps in between. 

Tweek stiffened.

*I don’t remember Craig helping me dress before.*

He watched Craig’s fingers— precise, patient. He’d seen that movement before. The way Craig was tugging on the shirt felt familiar. Tweek’s shoulders dropped.

*He did help me dress before. . . yeah, I remember.*

Tweek’s own tremors always made buttons a pain. He’d miss one or get frustrated halfway through and just give up. Whenever Craig used to sleep over as kids, he’d make a fuss and help Tweek with the buttons. But eventually, Tweek was able to do them himself. Regular shirts felt suffocating. With buttoned shirts, Tweek was able to choose how close he wanted it pressed against his neck.

“I lost time, didn’t I?” Tweek asked hesitantly. “Did I do something?”

“No,” Craig said gently. “You were just standing by your door with your shirt really wet. You’re ok.” He tugged at the shirt a little.

“Is that why you’re changing me?”

“Well, you started to take off your shirt.” Craig hesitated for a second before glancing up at Tweek. “I’ve learned it’s just easier to help you when this happens. If it bothers you—”

“N-no, no, it doesn’t,” Tweek lowered his head. “I just didn’t— didn’t know you helped me. Thank you for doing… this.”

Craig’s expression softened as he looked up at Tweek, his eyes shifting to a yellow-green, bright like hard candy.

“No problem. I’d rather help than have you walking around naked.”

“I’ve never done that\!” Tweek grabbed Craig’s arms. His voice pitched up. “Oh God— have you seen me naked?”

Craig chuckled and shook his head. Tweek let go and shoved Craig’s shoulder gently.

“I was kidding.” Craig lowered his head again. “You should clean your room today, honey.” He did the last button and adjusted the sleeves. “Want me to come help?”

“No. I can do it.” Tweek stepped back, away from Craig’s hands.

*I can’t bother him anymore.*

Craig nodded and walked to grab his black backpack from beside the closet and slipped it on. Tweek could see the tiredness in the way he walked to pick up the blue backpack by Tweek’s bed.

“Don’t forget to do your exercise,” Craig said as he handed him the blue backpack.

“Ok.” 

Tweek took the backpack from Craig and gripped the shoulder straps, not wanting to put it on yet. Craig pulled out a blue beanie from his pocket and slipped it on. 

*I need to ground myself.*

Every time Tweek lost time, the first exercise to do was always Grounding. He needed to stay in control. If he didn’t ground himself in the present, he would lose time again or could go into a rabbit hole. Sometimes, Grounding helped avoid losing time, so he knew the exercise well. 

Tweek took a deep breath.

*I don’t want to annoy him.* 

As they walked into the hallway, Tweek looked around. 

*Start with five things you can see*.

One. The dark wood of the floor leading down the stairs. 

Two. Craig’s eyes. Almond-shaped. Dark green— like clouds over a grassy field— as he looked up from the bottom of the stairs.

Three. The ugly fuchsia sofa against the wall. The matching carpet in front of it.

Four. Craig’s hoodie— dark, faded blue. His favorite.

Five. His mom’s dark pink lipstick as she smiled. 

She walked out of the kitchen with two plates. Her short brown hair curled at the edges, brushing against her jaw as she set the plates down on the table.

“Good morning\!” Manuela said. 

Tweek dropped his backpack under the table and sat down. He kept his breathing steady as he looked at the plate. Bacon, eggs and toast. 

*Next, four things you can touch*.

One. Toast.

Tweek reached for the toast. His mom sat across from him. Her elbows landed on the table, her hands spread flat. She was easy to reach. Something was telling him not to try. He didn’t want to touch her— not yet, not before he felt fully in control.

Craig returned and sat beside him, bringing two glass cups of orange juice and setting one gently in front of Tweek. 

Two. Glass of orange juice.

Three. Bacon.

He felt the crisp beneath his touch before he brought it to his mouth. Craig started to eat. Tweek reached over and placed a hand on Craig’s wrist— just long enough to feel the warmth. Craig kept eating without glancing towards Tweek.

Four. Craig.

*Three things you can hear.*

One. Fork.

The soft scrape of a fork against the plate.

Two. Clock.

The tick-tick-tick of the clock by the dining table.

DING\!— Tweek jumped slightly in his chair. It was his mom’s phone.

Three. Text alert. 

*Almost finished. Two things you can smell.*

One. Coffee.

Always coffee. It clung to the walls, soaked into the wood, and lived in the corners of his house.

Craig leaned over to nudge the fork closer. A soft floral scent drifted from him.

“Eat the eggs before they get cold,” Craig said.

*Lavender?*

As Craig went back to eat, Tweek leaned in and sniffed at him. The scent was clean, fresh.

Two. Lavender.

*Last step. One thing you can taste.*

Toast.

He took a bite. 

He didn’t notice his mom had been watching him. He jolted upright when their eyes met, heat rushing to his face.

*Oh geez. How long has she been staring at me?*

“Are you doing ok, sweetie?” she asked.

“Oh. . . uh. . .Yes.” He grabbed his fork. “I’m fine.”

“Grounding,” Craig said as he sipped the orange juice. “Tweek’s grounding himself.”

He set the glass back down and glanced at Tweek. There was a beat as she processed what she heard.

“Grounding?” she repeated, tilting her head. “Oh— oh, right. For the dissociation?” Her voice softened, like she’d only half-remembered what it meant.

She let her hands move closer towards Tweek. He leaned back.

*Please don’t touch me.*

Craig stood up, and the chair scraped against the floor. Tweek flinched at the sound.

“He just lost time. He’s finished his exercise.” Craig said flatly. “He’s fine.”

He walked back into the kitchen. Tweek looked at what he had left on his plate. He wasn’t very hungry. His appetite was always small. He wanted to finish before Craig got back in case they needed to leave already. He finished the whole plate quickly and chugged his orange juice.

“Slow down, sweetie.” Manuela leaned her chin against the palm of her hand. “You won’t be late for 10th grade.”

Tweek’s whole body jerked. The glass cup slammed on the table, making Manuela jump in surprise.

“What?” his shoulder twitched. The room tilted, just slightly. “Th-that’s today?” Craig returned with a paper plate in his hands and set it in front of Tweek. “That’s today?\!” He looked up at Craig. 

The tremors started— first in his fingers. Then his wrists. Then his arms. He clamped his hands together and lowered them on his lap, trying to hold the shaking in place. The food in his stomach felt heavy, a pressure making him nauseous. 

*Of course, the backpacks, the breakfast. Of course it’s today\!* 

“Could you give Stripe his breakfast?” Craig’s voice was soft, his hand brushed across Tweek’s back in a gentle pat. He turned and began to quietly stack the empty plates. Tweek rocked back and forth slowly.

“Oh no. Did I trigger something?” Manuela said sadly, with the kind of sadness reserved for burnt toast, not the panic spiraling out right in front of her. “It won’t be that bad, sweetheart. No need to be scared. Just some first-day jitters.” She smiled. “How about some coffee? Do you want me to get you a cup?”

Craig’s head snapped toward her, grip tightening on the plates— just enough for them to clink together.

“I don’t want to go. What if I forgot something? I don't remember what I needed. I don’t remember what to do.” Tweek’s hands flew up to the sides of his head, fingers tangling in his hair. “Did I get everything? What time does school start? Are we going to miss the bus?”

Tweek’s stomach felt like it was being pulled down. He could feel his mouth start salivating, preparing itself for the puke that was threatening to come up. Craig set the plates back down and crouched beside him, down at eye level. He placed both hands slowly on Tweek’s knees.

*I need to stop.*

Tweek’s breathing was shallow. His body felt like it would float away from itself. His face felt hot and his heart pulsed against his ears. Tweek’s eyes darted back and forth searching for something to focus on. Craig stayed silent and waited.

*Stop, stop. Stop it\! Just stop it\! Calm down already\!*

Tweek’s eyes finally met Craig’s gaze.

“Tweek, we triple-checked your backpack,” Craig said calmly. He took a deep breath, and Tweek mimicked him without thinking. His chest loosened. “You didn’t forget anything. Even if you did. . . we’ll just take it tomorrow.” Tweek nodded, still shaky. “Nothing will happen if you forget. You’re ok. Just breathe.”

“But— what if—.”

“We still have to go. We don’t have a choice.” Craig took another deep breath. “It will be the same as last year, ok?”

“Ok . . .ok.”

“School starts in forty-two minutes. The bus will be here in ten minutes. We have time.” 

“Yes. . . we have time. . . it’s ok.” 

Tweek let his hands fall on his lap. He took a steady deep breath. Craig gave his knees a soft squeeze. He repeated his question gently.

“Could you feed Stripe his breakfast?”

Tweek turned. The paper plate had small lettuce leaves, chunks of apple, and cut-up cucumber. Tweek slowly stood, grabbing the edge of the table. His stomach tightened, his head heavy, but the nausea was getting better. One more deep breath, then he grabbed the plate and walked upstairs. He wanted to hear Stripe squeak and pet him for a little while. Stripe always helped him calm down. 

Tweek walked into his room, stepping over the pile of kraft paper, bubble wrap, and boxes. It wouldn’t be easy to forget to clean— he had to climb over the small mountain of trash just to reach Stripe’s habitat. 

*No wonder Craig slept on the bed.* 

Stripe squeaked the second he saw Tweek, waddling around and doing a little jump as he got closer to where Tweek was standing. Stripe looked like a living puffball, so fat and round. His fur was mostly orange, with a crisp white line running straight down the center of his face, a matching band across his back, and a snow-colored belly. Stripe’s nose twitched and his mouth opened and closed as he squeaked with happiness. Tweek dropped the food into a small bowl next to the feeding dish and smiled as Stripe ran towards the lettuce leaf first.

“I wish I liked vegetables as much as you,” he murmured as he pet Stripe’s body. “Did you have a good workout yesterday after you escaped?” He poked at Stripe’s nose. “You can only be free when me or Craig are paying attention. I don’t want you to get lost or stuck somewhere again.”

Stripe looked up at him with a mouth working quickly on the lettuce leaf, cheeks bulging, entirely unbothered by Tweek’s words. Tweek giggled under his breath. He pulled open the top drawer and grabbed a handful of hay to place in the corner of the habitat. He added a few more food pellets in the feeding dish. He looked at the water bottle. They had filled it up halfway last night but it was completely full now. Craig had filled it already. Tweek nodded.

*Of course he did.* 

He watched as Stripe started on the cucumbers. Tweek's breathing had become stable. The chaos that was inside had subsided. He gave Stripe one last stroke across the head and turned to leave. He walked down the hall but before he could take the first step downstairs, he froze. Craig’s voice drifted up from below, sharp in the way it got when he was angry. He rarely raised his voice, but his tone shifted— cooled— like the air right before a storm. Tweek stood at the top of the stairs, his hand pressed flat against the wall. He tried to catch the words that were being said.

“. . . How does that make me ignorant?” Manuela asked.

“It’s not your fault for being ignorant,” Craig replied. “It’s only your fault for staying ignorant.”

His words were blunt and mean when he got angry.

“Craig, we’ve done what we can. We can’t just tiptoe around him all the time. He needs to see another therapist, it's been years now—”

Tweek let his arm slip against the wall as he lowered himself to sit at the top of the stairs. His shoulder rested against the plaster; his head settled quietly against the cold surface. 

*They’re at it again.*

Whenever Tweek wasn’t around they always had something to argue about. Craig never agreed with the way his parents tried to help him. Even before the accident, Craig always seemed disappointed with their efforts. It was after the accident that Tweek noticed the arguments started. The way Craig’s attitude shifted. His face tightened when he was around them. They only argued once in front of him. He couldn’t remember what happened after the argument. Only that, they never did it again. After that, Craig was polite and always respectful to his parents. At least while he was with Tweek. 

This was the first time he heard them argue about a therapist. He’d eavesdrop sometimes before walking back into the room. Tweek felt they were hiding something from him. He had hoped that it would slip but the arguments were always about exercises his parents needed to remember or trauma triggers. 

Deep down, Tweek was sure Craig hated them. Not that Craig would ever tell him. He always tried to hide his dislike, he would press his lips together or just walk away if Tweek’s parents started to get on his nerves. 

A small nausea crawled up his throat. The thing about Craig was that he didn’t hate people easily. People annoyed him easily, sure, but actual hate took work. Effort. Energy. Craig would rather spend doing literally anything else. There was a comfort in knowing that.

So if Craig felt that way about his parents. The reason behind it must be bad.

The thought made Tweek’s stomach tighten.

*I can’t hate them. They’re trying. I should just do better*. 

“He’s doing fine.” Craig’s voice trailed upstairs, tired now. “I help him if he needs it.”

“But that’s the point. You shouldn’t have to.”

“Are you going to handle it? The Rabbit holes? Triggers?”

“Craig—”

“I had to figure this out myself,” Craig said, his voice rising. “For weeks after the accident—”

“Because you wanted to—”

“No.” Craig interrupted quickly. “The last therapist you sent him to didn't help. Because of him Tweek almost—”

“Stop\!” Manuela’s hand slammed against the table. “Just... stop.”

There was a pause. Tweek hugged himself and closed his eyes. 

*I wish I just remembered everything. That would fix me, right?*

“You don't just dump him on a therapist and hope they fix him while you two do nothing.” Craig’s voice was lower, but steady.

“We’re not doing that,” she sighed. “We want him to get better.”

“Wanting stuff is easy, Manuela.” Craig's voice sharpened. “Actually doing it every day is the part you guys keep fucking up.”

*It’s my fault. I just need to do better.* 

“He’s our son, we do everything we can for him.” Her voice rose. “Look, we gave you what you wanted— time. Well, now it's our turn. We want him to see a therapist.”

Craig let out a short, annoyed scoff.

Tweek heard his mom inhale. He dropped his hands to grip the edge of the step.

“We think... it would be a good idea for you to join Tweek in therapy,” she said, calmer now.

“What? No.”

“Listen,” her voice dropped, low and serious— annoyed. “You both need the help. Have you looked at yourself lately? You look exhausted.” 

“It’s just been a few bad days. It’s always like that at the end of the summer.”

“You shouldn't be the only one in charge of this. You also need to talk to a—”

“My parents can’t pay for a therapist.”

Manuela cleared her throat.

“Well. . . that's the thing. Mr. Tweak and I are very grateful for everything you’ve done for Tweek during… well.…” her voice softened. “We want both of you to heal from all this and move on. So we’ll pay for both of you to go—”

“No.”

“Even if you don’t believe it, we’ve grown to care about you too. And—”

“I don't want you to pay for me.”

“Craig.” Her voice rose again, then fell quiet.  

Tweek imagined his mom taking a breath, resetting her tone. 

“This isn't going to get better. . . not without professional help. You’ve done what you can, but…” she let out a heavy sigh. “You’re still just a kid… we can’t keep robbing you of a childhood.”

Tweek let his head fall, his shoulders slumped down.

*I hate this.* 

When Craig spoke again, his voice had returned to its usual soft monotone.

“I help Tweek because I want to. Not for you. Not for Richard.” Craig shuffled with something— his backpack maybe. “If I go, it’s only because Tweek needs me to.”

Tweek swallowed hard trying to push down the guilt that was churning inside his stomach. Every time he decided to try and do things on his own. Something happened that left him useless. Craig had to come and save him. No matter what he tried, something inside him kept needing Craig to come back. 

*Craig’s a good friend… he doesn’t deserve this. Oh geez, I really suck.*

“We’ll talk to him tonight. I’m sure he’ll be happy to hear you get to come with him. We want you to get better too.”

“Right,” Craig said coldly. “Whatever you say.”

A chair scraped across the floor— Craig standing.

Tweek stood up slowly. His stomach twisted, a tight knot pulling upward toward his ribs. It felt uncomfortable but he took a few deep breaths to help. He walked back towards the hallway not wanting to get caught eavesdropping and counted a few seconds. 

*Ok, time to walk downstairs.* 

As he took the first step down he heard Craig’s voice. He paused and listened.

*It’s not over?* 

“Please remember,” Craig added, “don’t let Richard give him a ton of coffee in the mornings. It makes his anxiety worse.”

Tweek continued to walk downstairs. Craig walked towards the door holding Tweek’s backpack.

“Thank you for breakfast, Mrs. Tweak,” Craig said, opening the door. 

Tweek turned, giving his mom a small wave and a forced smile. He grabbed the backpack from Craig’s outstretched hand.

“Have a good day\!” she called as the door closed behind them.

 

The day was cool with a soft breeze that brushed against Tweek’s face and teased his hair as they stepped onto the sidewalk. The cold helped with his stomach pain. As they walked his shoulder twitched hard. 

*If they ask me tonight, I can text him later before going to bed*. 

Craig reached for his hand, letting his long, cold fingers slip between Tweek’s. Holding Craig’s hand was one of the only habits his brain didn’t throw away. It always felt easy and natural. 

*Wait… but I don't want to bother him more. He’s already done a lot*. 

Tweek sighed and bowed his head.

“Want to do some breathing exercises?” Craig asked, glancing over. Tweek jumped a little.

“Oh… no…I feel ok. M-Maybe. . . maybe once we get on the bus.”

Tweek's eyes wandered over the houses, the sidewalk— the same path as always. His mind slowed down. There were moments when things were good and the world wasn’t noisy. A few seconds, a few minutes where the world looked bright and new. His eyes looked at the brownish snow that clung to the edge of the street. The sound of birds as they flew above them. His eyes drifted from the clouds to Craig.

The dark blue beanie with the small NASA logo on the side. Tweek had taken a long time deciding what to buy Craig for his birthday. Tricia suggested the beanie.

*Oh yeah… he lost his other blue hat… When was that?* 

Tweek’s eyes fell on Craig’s thick eyebrows, dark lashes, and then his straight nose. Craig’s lips, naturally downturned at the corners, giving him the faintly unimpressed brooding look he always had. Lips neither full nor thin. Then Tweek saw a small scar on Craig’s chin that he never noticed before.

“Tweek?” Craig glanced at him and smiled.

That rare smile that no one else got to see. Everyone would see the smirk. A lot of female customers would mistake his smirk as flirting. Tweek’s dad preferred that since it made more female customers come back.

“Dude,” Craig said as he pinched Tweek’s nose.

“Huh?” Tweek jumped a little. “What?”

“You look like you want to eat me or something.”

“Ahh\! No, I don’t\!”

“You wanna wear it?”

“What?”

“Your ears are red.” Craig stopped and took off his beanie. "You're probably cold."

Before Tweek could protest, he placed the beanie on top of Tweek’s head. A soft scent of cotton tickled his nose— clean and fresh. The warmth from the beanie hugged his head. Craig adjusted it and looked at Tweek.

“I wasn’t losing time,” Tweek said defensively.

“Yeah, I know. Just spacing out a little.” He pinched Tweek’s cheeks together to force his lips to make a fish face. “Still. You doing ok?”

“Yup,” Tweek said in a muffled voice from his lips being pressed together. He moved Craig’s hand away. “I’m just nervous. . . .I feel like something bad will happen.”

“It might,” Craig ran his fingers through his hair. The strands falling perfectly in place. “But we got this.” He shrugged and slid his hand back into Tweek’s, naturally, like a habit. “Just another day. We did good last year, so we just gotta do the same.”

They started walking again. Tweek’s chest loosened slightly, and the tension in his stomach eased as the warmth settled around their joined hands.

“Right,” Twerk muttered. 

“Besides, something always happens to this stupid town,” Craig said as he shoved his other hand inside his hoodie pocket. “And anyway, Marla is going to our school. You said she was nice, right?”

“Yeah.” Tweek took a deep breath. “I hope she likes South Park.”

“No one likes South Park.”

“*You* don’t like South Park.” He bumped Craig’s shoulder and smiled at him. “Well, I hope she at least has a good first day of school.”

“You’re a better person than me, babe,” Craig said as he shook his head and smirked.

 

As they walked up to the bus stop, Clyde and Butters were already there. He didn’t get to hang out with them as much due to working at the coffeehouse. Tweek could see both of them hadn’t changed much over the summer. 

Clyde still had medium brown hair, tousled and messy. He wore his usual burgundy bomber jacket— blue collar and sleeves— paired with gray jeans and sneakers.

Butters had the same pale golden-blond hair, shaved on the sides with a soft flop over the top that leaned to one side. He was wearing his long-sleeve dark cyan shirt and dark army green cargo pants.

“What up, homos.” Clyde called out as they approached the curb.

*Yup, same Clyde.*
